# Resume standard

Receiving a resume does not authorize rewriting it. Ordinary intake is a quick
red-flag screen; comprehensive critique and revision are an active workflow that
begins only when the user asks to improve, rewrite, tailor, or otherwise change
the resume.

This standard evolves when a reusable resume-review or document-quality rule
improves. Candidate facts and requested positioning remain under `private/`.

## Intake: red flags only

Preserve the original, extract usable candidate evidence, and ask: **Are there
any material red flags in this resume?** A red flag is something likely to make
the document unusable, misleading, or materially harmful in screening, such as:

- unreadable or corrupted content, nonselectable text, severe rendering defects,
  or a layout that common ATS parsing cannot reasonably recover;
- a clear contradiction or impossible timeline involving employment, education,
  identity, or eligibility;
- a prominent claim that appears internally unsupported or misleading enough to
  create a serious credibility risk;
- missing or plainly broken essential contact or identity information;
- content that exposes sensitive information the candidate would not reasonably
  intend to place on a resume.

Word count, ordinary density, a page break, PDF metadata, stylistic preferences,
minor wording, an unconventional but readable section, or the absence of
external proof for every metric are not red flags by themselves. Do not ask the
candidate to reconfirm every date, title, skill, project, or metric during
ordinary intake.

If there are no material red flags, say so briefly and use the resume as-is. If
one exists, identify only the red flag and ask one targeted clarification at a
time. Do not silently revise the resume.

## Active improvement

When the user explicitly asks to improve the resume, perform a fuller review.

Look for:

- unsupported or internally inconsistent claims;
- unclear timelines, titles, ownership, or outcomes;
- dense wording, weak hierarchy, and unnecessary repetition;
- ATS-hostile layout, graphics, columns, headers, or contact information;
- important context that can be clarified with a factual question.

Distinguish facts already in the resume, facts verified in artifacts, facts the
candidate confirmed, and unknowns. Never turn an inference into a resume fact.

## Revision

Preserve the original under `private/resume/original/`. When the user requests
new files, make an editable DOCX the maintained source and derive the PDF from
it. Use black text, conventional section headings, ordinary bullets, selectable
text, and restrained spacing. Prefer a readable two-page resume to a compressed
one-page document.

Render and visually inspect every generated document. Confirm there is no
clipping, overlap, broken glyph, orphaned heading, or nearly empty final page.
