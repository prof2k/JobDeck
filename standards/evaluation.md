# Job-evaluation standard

Evaluate whether the candidate can perform the role's central daily work now or
after ordinary onboarding. Do not manufacture a transferable argument for every
job.

This standard evolves when a reusable evaluation rule improves. Candidate-only
preferences and corrections belong in `private/search-preferences.md` instead.

## Order of operations

1. Extract the role's identity and four to eight core requirements.
2. Apply explicit hard constraints: eligibility, location, arrangement,
   compensation floor, level, employment type, and user dealbreakers.
3. Match every core requirement to `Direct`, `Adjacent`, or `None` evidence.
4. Assign a fit class.
5. Score only to rank within the class.
6. Record gaps, risks, source evidence, and verification time.

## Fit classes

- **A - Ready:** direct alignment on role identity, level, and central work; no
  missing identity-defining requirement.
- **B - Adjacent:** most daily work is directly supported and one meaningful but
  learnable gap remains.
- **C - Stretch:** strong general alignment with one significant non-identity
  gap. Include sparingly and label clearly.
- **D - Exclude:** failed hard constraint, unsupported role identity, or multiple
  central gaps.

Scores communicate ordering, not certainty or response probability. Observable
priority signals may include evidence alignment, appropriate level, clear
eligibility, posting freshness, and limited critical gaps.

## Company application policies

Store a maximum-application rule only when an official source supports it.
Record the source URL, summary, verification date, limit, and time window. Treat
unverified information as an advisory, never a hard block.
